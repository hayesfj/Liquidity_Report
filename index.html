Macro Liquidity & Risk Appetite Report

Date: August 16, 2026 | Report #: 2026-08-16

Current Thoughts

The macro regime sits at Selective Risk-On (6.10/10). Institutional risk appetite is running hot — breadth is broad, cyclicals are leading, small caps are outperforming, and credit spreads remain compressed — but the liquidity plumbing is carrying a meaningful TGA headwind, and real yields at 2.40% technically sit in restrictive territory per the model. The S&P 500 just closed at a record high of 7,814.88 on August 14, powered by AI earnings, disinflating data (CPI +0.1% MoM, PPI flat for July), and a VIX that has declined for four consecutive weeks.

The key tension in this regime: equity vol is at multi-month lows (VIX 14.63) while real rates remain above the model's "caution" threshold. The market is discounting rate drag via strong earnings growth (S&P 500 tracking 20%+ Q2 EPS growth YoY for the seventh consecutive quarter of double-digit expansion). This is sustainable as long as earnings momentum holds, but it narrows the margin of safety.

Assets favored in this regime:

  • Industrials (XLI): Cyclical leadership confirmed; AI infrastructure capex and reshoring beneficiary
  • Energy (XLE): Leading YTD, commodity reflation theme intact
  • Small Caps (IWM): Outperforming SPY meaningfully — the clearest breadth signal in the model
  • Quality Growth (mega-cap tech/XLK): Still leading YTD despite real yield drag; earnings justify premium
  • Materials (XLB): Outperforming, broad participation signal

Assets to underweight or avoid:

  • Defensives (XLU, XLP): Structurally lagging in risk-on regime; yields compete with utilities
  • Long-duration bonds: Real yields restrictive, no near-term rate-cut catalyst
  • Speculative/unprofitable growth: Not favored at 2.40% real yields; profitability premium is real
  • Cash: Opportunity cost is rising; holding cash is increasingly a drag in a record-equities environment

Actionable Takeaways

What is working: Cyclicals broadly (XLI, XLB, XLE), small caps (IWM), equal-weight participation (RSP), quality tech/AI (XLK), credit carry (HYG). Breadth has been unusually strong — NYSE A/D line at all-time highs, 64% of S&P 500 above 50-DMA.

What is not working: Defensives (XLU +3.5%, XLP +6.6% YTD vs SPY +14%), long-duration Treasuries (IEF underperforming in a 2.40% real yield environment), communication services (XLC lagging at low single digits YTD), speculative growth.

Conditions that would upgrade to Full Risk-On (≥7.5/10): - Fed signals rate cuts, bringing 10Y real yield back toward 1.8–2.0% - TGA drawdown begins (fiscal spending cycle), releasing reserves back to system - VIX sustains below 13 on improved volume; MOVE falls below 65 - HY OAS compresses below 250 bps - DXY breaks below 98 (risk-appetite tailwind for commodities and EM)

Conditions that would trigger Risk-Off (≤3.5/10): - TGA surges further, bank reserves fall toward the $2.5T "red line" - 10Y real yields spike above 2.75% (fiscal/supply shock) - VIX breaks and holds above 20; MOVE crosses 100 - HY OAS widens above 350 bps (credit stress signal) - University of Michigan sentiment decline deepens into consumer spending data

Liquidity Analysis

  • Fed Balance Sheet (WALCL): $6.760T as of August 12, 2026. Up ~$108B year-over-year (+1.6% YoY). Stabilized after QT ended in early 2025; the Fed conducted ~$40B/month in reserve management T-bill purchases through April 2026.
  • Treasury General Account (WTREGEN): ~$997B as of August 11, 2026. Elevated and actively rising — climbed from ~$260B in early July 2025 to near $1T today following the administration's budget plan passage. This is the dominant reserve drain in the system.
  • Overnight Reverse Repo (RRPONTSYD): ~$0–1B — fully drained. The RRP peaked at $2.5T in late 2022 and was essentially exhausted by mid-2025. It no longer acts as a liquidity buffer or additional drain. The RRP cushion is gone.
  • Reserve Balances (WRESBAL): $2.993T as of August 5, 2026. Technically ample by historical standards, but the TGA expansion now directly reduces reserves with no RRP offset available.
  • Net Liquidity = WALCL − TGA − RRP: $6,760B − $997B − $1B = ~$5,762B. This is lower than 3–6 months ago due to TGA expansion.

Interpretation: The balance sheet is not the problem — it's stable to slightly expanding. The problem is the TGA building at pace near $1T. With the RRP buffer exhausted, every dollar added to the TGA now maps directly onto a dollar drained from bank reserves. Reserves at $2.993T remain above the estimated $2.5T "red line" where the Fed would feel pressure to act. But the cushion is narrowing.

Liquidity Score: 5.5 / 10

Financial Conditions Analysis

  • 10Y Real Yield (DFII10): 2.40% as of August 14, 2026. Per model: >2.3% = RESTRICTIVE. Real yields rose from ~1.99% in April 2026 to current levels. This is the single most important headwind to financial conditions. The 10Y nominal is at 4.68%, with breakevens near 2.21%.
  • DXY: 99.64 as of August 14, 2026. Per model: 98–101 = NEUTRAL. The dollar has been trending lower (was ~101 on July 29) as disinflation data removed the re-hike premium. The declining trend within neutral territory is a mild tailwind.
  • VIX: 14.63 as of August 14, 2026. Heading for a fourth consecutive weekly decline — longest run since May 2025. Bullish. Low equity vol signals institutional confidence in the near-term setup.
  • MOVE Index: 69.23 as of August 13, 2026. Well below the 80-threshold for "calm" bond markets. Bullish. Treasury option markets are not pricing any policy shock premium.
  • High Yield OAS: 271 bps as of August 12, 2026 (ICE BofA HY Index). Below the 350 bps caution level and far below the 600 bps stress threshold. Compressing from a temporary spike to ~340 bps in March 2026. HYG trading at $79.61, near 52-week range highs ($78.57–$81.36), yielding ~5.8%. Bullish.

Conflicting signal: Real yields are in the model's "restrictive" zone (2.40% > 2.30%) while every other financial conditions indicator is bullish. The market is tolerating restrictive real yields on the back of exceptional earnings growth. This is a healthy but fragile coexistence — any deterioration in forward earnings guidance would expose the real yield drag.

Financial Conditions Score: 5.5 / 10

Risk Appetite Analysis

  • HYG / IEF Relative Strength: HYG (high yield) outperforming IEF (7–10yr Treasuries). Spreads at 271 bps are near multi-year tights. HYG near its 52-week high while IEF faces headwinds from elevated rates. Risk appetite improving — RISK-ON signal.
  • RSP vs. SPY: RSP (equal weight) outperforming SPY (cap-weight) by approximately 2 percentage points YTD (~15.8% vs. ~14.0%). This confirms that the rally is NOT purely a mega-cap/AI narrow trade. Broad participation — BULLISH.
  • Cyclicals vs. Defensives (YTD, as of July 31): XLI +18.3%, XLB +15.0% vs. XLU +3.5%, XLP +6.6%. Cyclical outperformance spread of approximately 9–14 percentage points. XLE at +36.6% adds commodity cycle confirmation. Cyclicals leading — expansion signal.
  • IWM vs. SPY: IWM (Russell 2000) 1-year return of +36.9% vs. SPY 1-year return of +21.5%. YTD, IWM is meaningfully outperforming. Small caps only outperform when liquidity is perceived as adequate and the credit cycle is healthy. Strongest single risk appetite signal in the model — VERY BULLISH.
  • Market Breadth: ~64% of S&P 500 members above their 50-DMA; ~69% above their 200-DMA. NYSE Advance-Decline line at all-time highs as of early August. More new 52-week highs than lows. McClellan Oscillator briefly negative in July but now recovering with the broader tape. S&P 500 on pace for its strongest August in over 40 years. Breadth is healthy — BULLISH.
