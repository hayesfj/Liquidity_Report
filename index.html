Liquidity Analysis

  • Fed balance sheet (WALCL) is flat at ~$6,748.6B — QT has effectively ended, so the Fed is no longer actively draining, but it isn't adding either. Neutral, not bullish.
  • TGA is rising (~$962B, up ~$75B from February lows) — Treasury cash rebuild pulls reserves out of the system dollar-for-dollar. This is the key bearish mechanical driver right now.
  • RRP is drained to ~$0.2B — the reverse-repo buffer that previously absorbed TGA swings is gone. TGA rebuilds now hit bank reserves directly, removing a shock absorber.
  • Reserve balances at $2,993.3B — still marginally above the ~$3T "ample" zone but trending down as TGA fills. Worth watching closely; this is where funding stress would first appear.
  • Net Liquidity ≈ $5,786.7B, flat-to-declining. Not contractionary enough to be risk-off, but no longer the tailwind it was earlier in the year.

Regime Classification: SELECTIVE RISK-ON

The internals (credit, breadth, small caps, cyclicals) are risk-on, but the macro plumbing and rates backdrop are not confirming. This is a market where participation is broadening even as the liquidity tailwind fades and real yields stay restrictive — a "risk-on carried by internals, capped by macro" configuration rather than a clean full-throttle regime.

Current Thoughts

Favored assets: - Cyclicals / industrials / financials — participating and confirmed by breadth rotation - Small caps (quality tilt) — leading, though this leans on a Fed-easing thesis that is now contested (see conflict below) - Quality growth — supported by calm vol, but capped by 2.39% real yields compressing long-duration multiples

Favored with caution / underweight: - Speculative growth — restrictive real yields are the wrong backdrop for the longest-duration equities - Defensives (XLU/XLP) — lagging; no reason to overweight while breadth is broad - Cash — reasonable as dry powder given asymmetric credit and thin liquidity buffer, but not a full defensive posture

Conflicting signals (explicit): 1. Rates direction vs. small-cap leadership. Small caps are rallying on a Fed-cut thesis, but the Fed's actual debate is between hold and hike. Chair Warsh's FOMC held 9–3 in July with three hawkish dissents; markets price ~44% odds of a September hike after the weak July jobs report (−23K payrolls, −103K revisions, wages +3.2%). If the Aug 12 CPI runs hot, the small-cap/breadth trade is directly exposed. 2. Tight credit vs. thin liquidity buffer. HY spreads price "perfection" (~273 bps) exactly as the RRP buffer hits zero and TGA drains reserves — benign until it isn't, with little cushion. 3. Calm vol vs. restrictive real yields. VIX/MOVE say all-clear; DFII10 says conditions are still tight.

Actionable Takeaways

What's working: - Broadening participation — equal-weight and small caps leading cap-weight - Cyclical/financial leadership over defensives - Benign credit and suppressed cross-asset volatility supporting carry and risk positioning

What's not working: - Net liquidity has stopped rising; TGA rebuild is a live reserve drain - Restrictive 2.39% real yields cap multiple expansion, especially for speculative growth - Zero RRP buffer removes the system's shock absorber - The rate-cut narrative underpinning small-cap leadership is not supported by current Fed posture

What would upgrade to Full Risk-On: - 10-Yr real yield back below ~2.0% - Clear dovish Fed pivot (cuts back on the table) — requires soft Aug 12 CPI - Net liquidity turning higher (TGA drawdown resuming and/or reserves stabilizing above $3T) - DXY breaking below 98 - Breadth expanding decisively above ~70% of stocks over 50-DMA

What would trigger Risk-Off: - HY OAS widening through ~350–400 bps (credit leads) - Reserve balances falling toward funding-stress levels with RRP already empty - Hot CPI forcing the Fed to hike in September (real yields spike, small-cap trade unwinds) - VIX/MOVE breaking out of their dormant ranges - RSP and IWM rolling back under SPY (breadth failure / return to narrow leadership)
